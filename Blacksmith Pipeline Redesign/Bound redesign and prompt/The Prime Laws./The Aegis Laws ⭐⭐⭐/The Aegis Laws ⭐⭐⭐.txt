# Foundational Axioms for Trustworthy Computation

## Axiom 1 — Evidence

**No claim is established by assertion alone. A claim requires evidence appropriate to the confidence demanded of it.**

An assertion, regardless of its source, does not establish truth or trust. Claims become eligible for acceptance only through the production of sufficient supporting evidence.

---

## Axiom 2 — Independence

**Evidence has no authority unless it can be independently evaluated.**

Evidence is trustworthy only when its validity can be assessed independently of the entity that produced the claim or the evidence itself. Trust cannot arise from self-validation.

---

### Consequence

These axioms do not prescribe implementation. They define the minimum conditions under which a computational claim may become trustworthy. Any trustworthy computational system must therefore provide:

* Evidence sufficient for the claim being made.
* A means for that evidence to be independently evaluated.

All higher-level laws, protocols, and implementations should be derivable from these axioms.
